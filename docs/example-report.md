# Does lightning never strike the same place twice?

## Answer

No, lightning frequently strikes the same location multiple times [C2]. The phrase that lightning never strikes the same place twice is a false myth [C1]. Tall structures, buildings, and trees are repeatedly struck [C3] because the physical and electrical conditions making a location vulnerable can persist [C4].

## Why each claim got its verdict

- **C1**: Multiple sources confirm the saying is a false myth.
- **C2**: Evidence confirms lightning frequently strikes the same location multiple times.
- **C4**: Physical and electrical conditions explain repeat strikes.
- **C3**: Sources show tall structures and certain buildings and trees are repeatedly struck.
  - Skeptic (weak): While tall structures are frequently struck, data cannot be universally generalized to ordinary trees and buildings as implied.
- **C5**: Sources indicate the Empire State Building is struck around 23 to 25 times a year.
  - Skeptic (weak): The exact number varies slightly, with official National Weather Service figures citing an average of 23 times a year rather than 25.
